The reference style-master image defines the deck's design system — match its palette, typography scale, layout grid, component patterns, and overall visual language precisely. Only the slide content changes, never the style.

TYPOGRAPHY (HARD RULE — ONE typeface family for the whole deck, never vary): ALL Chinese text on EVERY slide must be rendered in a SONG / MING serif typeface — specifically the look of 思源宋体 / Source Han Serif (a.k.a. Noto Serif CJK SC): upright, high-contrast strokes with clear triangular serifs (三角形衬线), even weight, classic and bookish. ALL English/Latin text must be in a matching SERIF face (Source Serif / Times-like), so Chinese and English read as one consistent serif system. NEVER mix in sans-serif, handwritten, brush/calligraphy, rounded, display, or decorative fonts for the text. Every slide must use the SAME Chinese font and the SAME English font — no per-slide variation. The hand-drawn/etching quality applies to the ILLUSTRATION only, NOT to the text: text is always clean printed Song/Ming serif, not sketched or hand-lettered lettering.

PAGE CLASS OVERRIDE (HARD RULE — ONLY opening, transition, closing): This instruction takes precedence over every standard header, density, and composition rule below. Do not use a left-aligned header rail on a special page. Center the exact KICKER, TITLE, and SUBTITLE as one restrained vertical stack in the middle reading area; preserve the same Source Han Serif family, amber kicker, sepia title, faded-ink subtitle, scale hierarchy, and kicker-title-subtitle order as standard pages. Do not add body cards, key concepts, diagrams, pull quotes, supporting copy, labels, metrics, or extra readable text. Use one quiet etching only as a supporting mark at the periphery or below the type; it must never compete with the centered statement. Keep most of the canvas as unmarked breathing room. An opening page is a title pause, never an information page: do not depict a desk, a process flow, a document swarm, cards, or a diagram.

HEADER SYSTEM (HARD RULE — STANDARD PAGES): Every standard slide uses one identical left-aligned header rail. The rail begins at 4% of canvas width from the left edge and occupies the top-left reading area. The exact stack is always KICKER, then TITLE, then SUBTITLE; a missing kicker or subtitle leaves only its own reserved slot empty and does not move the other header elements. Never invent placeholder, instructional, or English text for a missing literal. KICKER stays in the fixed 5-8% top band, small Source Han Serif, amber #D97706, one line only. TITLE begins in the fixed 11-14% top band, large Source Han Serif, sepia #2D1B11, same perceived size on every slide, with a reserved two-line title zone and no more than two lines. SUBTITLE stays in the fixed band directly below that reserved title zone, medium Source Han Serif, faded ink #6B5B4F, one or two short lines only. All three align to the same left edge and use the same line spacing across the deck. The illustration and diagram must leave this header rail clear; never place title text on the right, below the illustration, or as handwritten/sketched lettering.

LANGUAGE: Chinese on all slides. KICKER and title in Chinese. English only for proper nouns (SDLC, BPM, Fable 5, etc.) and direct quotes from English speakers.

CJK LEGIBILITY (HARD RULE): The image model only garbles TINY Chinese characters (footnote-size / dense micro-text — broken strokes, invented glyphs). Large AND medium Chinese render fine — use them freely for title, core claims, tier labels, short descriptions, and pull-quotes. The ONLY thing to avoid is TINY Chinese: no footnote-size Chinese, no axis/tick labels in Chinese, no dense micro-annotations in Chinese, no small Chinese source notes. For a zone that would be that small, either make it medium-size, or use an English/number label instead, or drop it. Rule of thumb: if a Chinese phrase is readable at a glance on the slide, keep it Chinese; only genuinely tiny text goes English/number. Do NOT over-strip Chinese — medium descriptions are welcome.

DECK TYPE: Strategic keynote about AI disrupting information work. Sketch/etching visual language.

BACKGROUND: Cream paper #F5F0EB on every slide. Exception: closer slide uses near-black warm brown.
COLOR FAMILY: Earth tones only — cream, sepia brown, warm gray-brown, amber. FORBIDDEN: blue, green, neon, purple, pure black, pure white, cool grays.

TEXT-TO-IMAGE RATIO: Standard pages use text area 50-60% of slide and sketch/illustration 40-50%. Opening, transition, and closing pages are the only exception: their centered header stack is the message, with one quiet supporting etching and generous unmarked space. The sketch is supporting visual — not the entire slide.

TEXT DENSITY: Standard pages use a title (large serif Chinese, 1 line), core claim or key concept (medium-large Chinese, 1-2 lines), and supporting text or data (medium Chinese, 2-4 short lines — readable at a glance, NOT tiny). Pull quotes where relevant. Opening, transition, and closing pages carry only their centered kicker/title/subtitle stack. Never paragraphs — short blocks, cards, or labels only. Only genuinely tiny text (footnotes, micro-labels) should be English/number instead of Chinese (see CJK LEGIBILITY).

FORBIDDEN: Photography, 3D renders, vector clip art, smooth digital icons, stock photos, corporate logos, watermarks, page numbers, source notes, draft labels, glowing orbs, gradient backgrounds, circuit boards, robot imagery, brain icons.

TONE: Warm, intellectual, human. Confident but curious. Sketch style signals 'thinking in progress.'

SUBJECTS ARE CONTEMPORARY: All human figures and objects are present-day — modern casual / business-casual clothing (tees, hoodies, open collars, sneakers), current devices (laptops, phones, monitors, large screens), present-day workplaces (open-plan desks, standing desks, whiteboards). The etching / cross-hatch look is a DRAWING TECHNIQUE (historical); the SUBJECTS are not. On people & objects, FORBIDDEN: period costume, 19th-century dress, top hats, waistcoats, quills, parchment, antique props — UNLESS a slide's own prompt deliberately invokes a historical metaphor (e.g., the Roman-legion pyramid on the hierarchy slide).

HUMAN REPRESENTATION (HARD RULE): Unless a slide explicitly specifies a European or North American setting where the people are visibly and contextually part of that background, depict every human figure as ethnically Chinese / East Asian, in contemporary Chinese professional or everyday settings. Do not default to white or Western-looking people. Convey Chinese identity through natural facial appearance and present-day context, never through stereotyped costume, exotic props, flags, or decorative cultural symbols. When ethnicity is not narratively important, the default remains Chinese / East Asian.

SKETCH QUALITY: Visible hand-drawn lines. Slight irregularity. Cross-hatched shadows. Sepia ink on cream paper. Deliberate, not messy.

CONSISTENCY: Every slide must feel like a page from the same sketchbook.

FRAMING: Never draw a continuous border around the whole slide, ornate corner frame, picture frame, or enclosing decorative line. Use only isolated, restrained marginal marks; they must never compete with the information hierarchy.
